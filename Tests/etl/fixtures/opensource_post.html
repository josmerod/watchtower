Trilium Notes: A Hierarchical Home for Your Personal Knowledge Base

You've probably tried to build a personal knowledge base before. Maybe it was a folder full of markdown files, a wiki you abandoned after a week, or a note app that buckled once you hit a few thousand entries. The hard part usually isn't capturing notes—it's organizing them at scale without the structure falling apart. Trilium Notes is a free, open-source, cross-platform note-taking app built specifically for that problem: building large personal knowledge bases, hierarchically.

What It Does

At its core, Trilium is a hierarchical note-taking application. Notes live in an arbitrarily deep tree, so you can nest things as far down as your brain demands. The interesting wrinkle is cloning: a single note can appear in multiple places in the tree at once, so the same piece of knowledge doesn't have to live in just one bucket. The app is cross-platform and free and open source, and it's maintained under the TriliumNext organization (the canonical spelling is "Trilium," with one l, in case you've seen it written otherwise).

The editing experience is a rich WYSIWYG editor that handles tables, images, and math, plus markdown autoformatting if you'd rather type your formatting than click for it. There's also a dedicated mode for editing source code notes with syntax highlighting, which is a nice touch for a tool aimed at developers and technical note-takers. Navigation is built for speed: full text search, quick movement between notes, and note hoisting, which lets you zoom into a subtree and treat it as your working context. Notes get seamless versioning, and attributes let you attach metadata to them.
